3. End-to-end launch

  ros2 launch sim_fast fixed_wing_demo.launch.py start_delay_sec:=2.0

  Nodes:
    fixedwing_backend_adapter_node  - 6-DOF fixed-wing actuator plant
    fixed_wing_guidance_node        - Aerial Kit L1 + TECS + attitude PID
    fixed_wing_demo_node            - Command, enable and 3-waypoint mission

  Result:
    first waypoint   [97.5, 48.4, 54.3]
    second waypoint  [208.3, 62.3, 54.4]
    third waypoint   [257.3, -28.5, 54.5]
    mission complete at [257.30, -28.46, 54.49]
    final horizontal distance 10.51 m

  The wing started at 55 m, 15 m/s. Its altitude stayed within 0.53 m through
  all three waypoint transitions.

4. Gazebo visual twin

  ros2 launch sim_gazebo fixed_wing_gazebo_demo.launch.py \
    headless:=true start_delay_sec:=2.0

  Gazebo 8.10.0 started the `uav_fixed_wing` world and loaded
  `model://twin_wing_visual`. The ROS -> Gazebo set-pose service bridge was
  active at /world/uav_fixed_wing/set_pose, and the pose node reported:

    Gazebo twin-wing pose stream established

  The same L1/TECS flight loop completed:
    first waypoint               [97.6, 48.5, 54.3]
    second waypoint              [208.5, 62.3, 54.4]
    third waypoint               [257.3, -28.5, 54.5]
    mission complete             [257.25, -28.55, 54.47]
    final horizontal distance    8.95 m

  A live `gz model -m twin_wing -p` query during the run reported the model
  away from its spawn point at [204.067, 16.045, 40.742], confirming that the
  Gazebo entity was being driven rather than remaining at 55 m.

  The SDF is an FRD-oriented kinematic visual asset. Aerial Kit's
  FixedWingDynamics remains the authoritative 6-DOF plant; Gazebo supplies the
  scene and the visual wing, elevon, fin and prop-disc geometry. This is not a
  native Gazebo aerodynamic simulation.

  The asset tests parse the world and model SDF and pin the pose-service launch
  wiring. They do not replace the end-to-end launch above.

5. What was found and fixed

  The first run used the generic 3 N trim throttle while the simulated plant's
  level-flight trim at 15 m/s is 0.883 N. The aircraft accelerated and climbed
  roughly 15 m. The backend now uses `FixedWingDynamics.compute_trim()` and
  adds the computed elevon trim to the commanded pitch moment. The same demo
  then held near 55 m.

  Horizontal waypoint acceptance is used intentionally: TECS owns altitude,
  and a vertical transient must not keep a wing orbiting a point it has already
  overflown. The desired altitude is still sent to TECS on every update.

6. Not done
