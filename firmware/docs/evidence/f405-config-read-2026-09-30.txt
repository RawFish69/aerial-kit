AerialKit F405, WeAct board - the raw flash read of the configuration's sector

Taken 2026-09-30 with the board in its ROM bootloader, entered by hand at the
board (BOOT0 held, reset tapped). Tool: dfu-util 0.11, unpacked from the Ubuntu
archive with `apt-get download dfu-util` + `dpkg -x` into the job tmp - not
installed system-wide, and no root was used.

This is the read that trap 216 left open. The answer is below: the first word of
0x080C0000 is AK_FLASHLOG_MAGIC, which is a previous owner's flash-log header and
not a configuration at all.

Every read here is an UPLOAD. Nothing was written, erased or programmed, and no
command in this file carries `:leave` or a download. The board was left in DFU.

Device as the host saw it, on every read:
  Bus 001 Device 064: ID 0483:df11 STMicroelectronics STM Device in DFU Mode

dfu-util's own handshake, on the first read:
  Device DFU version 011a
  DFU state(10) = dfuERROR, status(10) = Device's firmware is corrupt
  Clearing status
  DFU state(2) = dfuIDLE, status(0) = No error condition is present
  Device returned transfer size 2048
  DfuSe interface name: "Internal Flash  "

Each read is the same command with a different address:

  dfu-util -d 0483:df11 -a 0 -s <address>:<length> -U <file>

ADDRESSING IS CHECKED BEFORE ANYTHING IS BELIEVED FROM IT
  A read of the vector table has to look like one: word 0 a stack pointer in
  SRAM, word 1 a reset handler in flash.
    0x08000000  20020000 0800F829 0800F825 0800F80D 0800F825 0800F825 0800F825 00000000
  w0 = 0x20020000 is the top of the part's 128 KB of SRAM and w1 = 0x0800F829
  is in flash, so the addresses are being honoured. Two further controls agree:
  0x080F0000 and 0x0801F000 come back blank, 0x08020000 does not.

0x0801F000 - THE TRACE PAGE, the last page of the image's sector
  All 4096 bytes 0xFF. Nothing has been saved there, so on this board the lamp
  is the only instrument and section 6b is the reading.
  sha256 f47a8ec3e9aff2318d896942282ad4fe37d6391c82914f54a5da8a37de1300c6

0x08020000 - THE FLASH LOG'S OWN FIRST SLOT, for comparison
  414B464C 003C0002 00000000 00000022 00169B28 00000000 00000000 DF8E2838
  w0 = 0x414B464C is AK_FLASHLOG_MAGIC ("AKFL"), w1 = 0x003C0002 carries
  AK_FLASHLOG_VERSION 2 in its low half. 1024 of 1024 bytes non-FF.
  sha256 1a32aefae29a890be1848ddf967a494da20a19bb1681640c0e9c4901d83c5ea9

0x080C0000 - THE CONFIGURATION'S BANK 0, SECTOR 10 - THE READ THAT WAS MISSING
  414B464C 003C0002 00000005 00000012 0005AC8A 00000000 00000000 4ADE7925
  w0 = 0x414B464C = AK_FLASHLOG_MAGIC, the same value the log's own first slot
  carries two sectors below it. w8 = 0x414B4653 = AK_FLASHLOG_SLOT_MAGIC
  ("AKFS"). So sector 10 holds a log header and a log slot: the previous owner
  of the region is still in it.
  4095 of 4096 bytes non-FF; 3333 of them are 0x00.
  sha256 c6fc7a83525435d752a8668bc8b92239a8b382b5d9fe0612167b0748c1758bd0

  And the reader's own test, run against these bytes rather than against a
  model of them:
      board sector 10 word 0 : 0x414B464C
      AK_CONFIG_MAGIC        : 0x414B4346   -> not equal
      erased (0xFFFFFFFF)?   : False
  so the sector is neither erased nor a configuration, which is the "not
  erased, nothing valid" branch, and exactly the message the board prints.

0x080E0000 - BANK 1, SECTOR 11 - NOT BLANK, AND THAT WAS NOT EXPECTED
  67120883 C4980462 B4A2086E E2E8D0D8 86042123 084C75CD 4D44193A 112F5A11
  sha256 32599f6d0e225bc53c9e55483b2ca0d660a367a33f9864f9eb5bd2dd380dd0f4
  4093 of 4096 bytes non-FF; all 256 possible byte values appear. Read twice, the
  two files are byte-identical (that same sha256 both times), so this is flash
  content and not a buffer or a transport artefact.

  How far it goes, 0x400 at a time:
    0x080E0000  non-FF 4093/4096
    0x080E1000  non-FF 1024/1024
    0x080E2000  non-FF 1022/1024
    0x080E4000  non-FF    0/1024
    0x080E8000  non-FF    0/1024
    0x080EC000  non-FF    0/1024
  The first 12 KB of the 128 KB sector is written and the rest is erased -
  three 4 KB slots' worth - and not one of the three known magics appears
  anywhere in it.

WHAT IS NOT ESTABLISHED
  What wrote the 12 KB in sector 11, or what it is. It carries none of
  AK_CONFIG_MAGIC, AK_FLASHLOG_MAGIC or AK_FLASHLOG_SLOT_MAGIC, its byte
  distribution is as flat as a random one, and it reproduces. That is the whole
  of it: not erased, not this firmware's configuration, and its origin has not
  been measured. Trap 216's bench prediction was that bank 1 is "the other,
  erased bank"; on this board that premise does not hold, so what a `save` would
  do here is not predicted by that paragraph and has not been tried.

0x080D0000 - THE SECOND 4 KB OF BANK 0
  00000000 00000000 00000000 00000000 414B4653 D79FFF87 000901AA 00000000
  1023 of 1024 bytes non-FF and word 0 is 0x00000000: a block of zeros, which is
  what a programmed region looks like rather than an erased one.
